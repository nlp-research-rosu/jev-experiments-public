# Methods review after the contrast-scaling pause

Reviewed 2026-09-21. Training remains paused at 1,692 complete family updates. This document recommends later experiments; it neither resumes training nor changes the approved scaling experiment. Only this report was written. No model execution, GPU/API inference, training, package installation, core/data/model edit, or Git operation was performed for this review. The frozen contrast test, quarantined Jev contrast results, and response archives were not read.

## Decision and evidence

The smallest useful sequence was correctness audit plus saved-logit temperature calibration. Those diagnostics now identify a substantial calibratable sharpness component and a concrete new-domain Score coverage mismatch; see the addendum below. **For semantic accuracy, prioritize one bounded hard-label coverage correction before changing the loss.** A short matched direct-loss comparison remains an alternative later probability-quality question. Genuine distribution targets are a subsequent data experiment. Explicit consistency is conditional on demonstrated relation violations. RLCR, Rewarding Doubt, and literal ConfTuner implementations are lower-priority alternatives for a different, generative interface.

This preserves the judgment in the earlier [calibrated-training research](../CALIBRATED_TRAINING_RESEARCH.md): neither hard-label supervision nor a non-RL optimizer has been shown to impose the observed ceiling. This review rechecked that note's paper formulas against full primary texts, including methods and theorem sections, rather than treating abstracts as implementation specifications.

The [GPU validation report](../contrast-scaling-v1/mid-gpu-validation/RESULTS.md) supports two distinct diagnoses:

| Observed validation evidence | Supported conclusion | Still a hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy rises from 555/800 initially to 631/800 at 200 families; it is 629/800 at 1,692. | Early training learned useful behavior; later exposure has not produced a comparable gain on this validation population. | A fundamental data-size, supervised-learning, or 2B-capacity ceiling. |
| From 200 to 1,692, NLL rises 0.7857 → 1.5498, while Brier rises 0.3357 → 0.3848. | Later probability quality is worse despite almost unchanged accuracy. | Whether sharpness alone explains all of the increase. |

These are repeatedly inspected validation results from one seed. The repeat-data compute control is unfinished. More unique families, more updates, and more replay exposure changed together; the present curve does not isolate them. The current correctness audit must determine whether any apparent method failure is actually a rendering, oracle, grouping, or annotation defect before a training ablation is interpreted.

## What the numerical model actually learns

The model readouts produce scalar FP32 logits from shared backbone representations. Noul has one binary readout, including a bias. Choice and Score use a shared compatibility readout across their candidate units. The readout initializer uses the original language model's A-minus-B projection; that initialization does not establish a calibrated semantic probability scale. The current scaling run starts from already-trained v0.2 adapters and heads, so attributing the present behavior directly to the fresh initializer would be unsupported.

The actual scaling loss is in _group_loss, with complete-group microbatching and equal means over new/old × Noul/Choice/Score in backward_family_loss:

\[
p_{\mathrm{Noul}}=\sigma(z),\qquad
p_i=\operatorname{softmax}(z)_i\quad\text{for Choice/Score},
\]

\[
L_{\mathrm{CE}}(p,q)=-\sum_i q_i\log p_i,
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{update}}=\tfrac16\sum_{o\in\{new,old\},\,t\in\{N,C,S\}}
\operatorname{mean}_{g\in(o,t)}L_g.
\]

For Noul, the equivalent two-outcome vector is `[1-p_true, p_true]`, and binary cross-entropy is computed directly from its logit. Candidate rows are not independent training labels: Choice/Score normalization and loss couple each complete question. All 20 new judgments plus one old question per primitive participate in an update. Thus the existing supervised objective already trains probabilities; adding RL is not required to obtain a differentiable proper loss.

target_vector already accepts Noul `probability_true`, keyed Choice vectors, and ordered Score vectors. The generic bundle_loss also contains invariant/complement consistency terms, but **the contrast-scaling training path does not use those terms**. The existence of that helper is not evidence that this experiment tested a pair loss. The [scaling contract](../../design/contrast-scaling-contract-v1.md) explicitly fixed hard BCE/CE with relations used for coverage/evaluation.

## Verified paper distinctions

Use `a` for correctness of a generated answer, `c` for its separately reported confidence, and `r_j` for probability of emitting a confidence token. These differ from OpenJev's semantic outcome vector `p`.

| Primary source and location checked | Actual method/formula | Consequence for OpenJev |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Guo et al., 2017, §4.2, Eq. 9](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a/guo17a.pdf) | Fit positive scalar `T` by validation NLL; use `softmax(z/T)`. | Minimal diagnostic. Noul uses `sigmoid(z/T)`. Each question's argmax is preserved; rubric reasoning is unchanged. |
| [Gneiting & Raftery, 2007, §3.1, Examples 1 and 3](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf) | Logarithmic and full categorical quadratic/Brier scores are strictly proper. | A positive weighted sum remains proper in expected loss. Finite-sample generalization, label validity and optimization remain separate requirements. |
| [Hinton et al., 2015, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.02531) | Match teacher and student distributions at common distillation temperature; optionally combine with hard-label CE at temperature one. The paper scales the soft term by `T²` to approximately compensate temperature-dependent gradient magnitudes. | Distillation transfers a teacher's function. It does not certify that teacher probabilities are truth. Distillation temperature and post-hoc calibration temperature serve different purposes. |
| [RLCR, v2, §3, Eq. 8 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2#S3) | `R = a - (c-a)²` for a generated answer and separate confidence; generative training uses GRPO. For answer success probability `s`, the expected reward is `s²-(c-s)²`, maximized at `c=s` and then by the most likely correct answer. | This incentive result assumes correctness feedback and the stated reward. It is not a convergence/generalization guarantee. OpenJev can evaluate all semantic outcomes directly, so a rollout policy is extra machinery. |
| [Rewarding Doubt, v6, §3.3 and §4, Response Generation](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02623v6) | `R = a log(c)+(1-a)log(1-c)`, with clipping/rescaling in implementation. PPO optimizes confidence generation after the answer has been generated and treated as fixed input. | Evidence for confidence estimation, not joint improvement of answer selection. The sampling action is a confidence token sequence, not OpenJev's semantic probability vector. |
| [ConfTuner, §3.2–3.3, Eq. 1 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18847#S3) | Tokenized Brier is `Σ_j r_j(a-j/N)²`. The population objective concentrates token probability on the confidence value nearest the answer's correctness probability. | **Not** categorical Brier `Σ_i(p_i-y_i)²` over semantic outcomes. A numerical head bypasses this token representation; no need to generate “80%” to learn 0.8. |
| [Reward-function analysis, July 2026, §5–6 and App. C](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04332v1) | Analyzes incentive conditions and tests confidence rewards with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/Dr GRPO. Joint answer/confidence optimization can favor confidently reported wrongness under unsuitable rewards. | Recent preprint supporting reward audits. Its generated-answer pathology does not invalidate full-vector supervised CE with a fixed gold event. |
| [Adaptive Temperature Scaling, §4, Eq. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19817#S4) | A hidden-state head predicts a shared positive scaling factor per next-token context. Its loss uses a hard target when the original prediction is correct and a uniform target when wrong. | A separate adaptation, not ordinary scalar NLL calibration. Defer the extra head. If adapted, share one temperature across a semantic question; independent candidate temperatures could change rankings. |

Official implementation documentation was also checked through the existing RLCR snapshot, ConfTuner snapshot, and full-text snapshots listed in the source manifest. The [RLCR repository](https://github.com/damanimehul/RLCR) describes rollout infrastructure and confidence-exploration instability; the [ConfTuner repository](https://github.com/liushiliushi/ConfTuner) describes confidence-token data generation and correctness assessment. They are not drop-in replacements for the current numerical interface. No repository code was installed or executed.

## Preserve event semantics and target provenance

The [probability-design note](../PROBABILITY_DESIGN.md) remains the governing distinction:

- **Noul:** predict the specified proposition under its evidence and criteria. “The event occurred” and “the authorized record establishes occurrence” are different events. Missing records do not automatically make occurrence false or imply probability .5. A closed-world evidentiary rule can give a perfectly deterministic false answer to the second question. Uncertainty from model limitations also differs from randomness in a supplied data-generating process.
- **Choice:** distribute mass over the declared mutually exclusive answer meanings. `Unknown` is an outcome when the rubric says the evidence is insufficient; a well-informed model can assign it probability near one. It is not an instruction to spread probability evenly. Source-admissibility mistakes need explicit matching/mismatching authority, time, entity and action controls with the same evidence otherwise held fixed.
- **Score:** retain the categorical vector over the supplied ordered levels. Score is not a verbal confidence scale. Keep modal-level accuracy, expected-level MAE and full-vector NLL/Brier distinct. Positive temperature preserves the mode but changes the mean. Different distributions can have the same mean, and equal index spacing need not mean equal real-world severity increments.

For a genuine stochastic fixture, a balanced latent state with a sensor that is 80% sensitive and has a 20% false-positive rate yields posterior .8 after a positive reading. That only supplies a target for the hidden-state event when the question and visible model make that event explicit. It does not supply .8 for “the sensor proves the event conclusively.” Exact posterior targets also require the prior to be available or part of the declared task distribution.

For ordinal diagnostics, optionally report normalized ranked probability score,

\[
\mathrm{RPS}(p,y)=\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-2}
\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}p_i-\mathbf1[y\le k]\right)^2,
\]

using only the rubric's declared order. This is an evaluation addition, not a proposal to replace CE with mean regression. The cumulative-distribution scoring construction is covered by [Gneiting & Raftery, §4.2](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf). Never enforce equality of Score means across two rubrics unless their level meanings actually map that way.

For soft targets, use `CE(q,p) = -Σ_i q_i log p_i`; minimizing it is equivalent to forward `KL(q || p)` up to target entropy. Report the full-vector Brier convention consistently: Noul's two-class sum is `2(p_true-q_true)²`, not the scalar convention used by many confidence papers. An arbitrary smoothing target `(1-ε)y+ε/K` is a regularization control, not measured uncertainty. Teacher token likelihood, verbal confidence, ensemble sampling frequency, and an externally justified semantic posterior must keep separate provenance labels.

## A bounded later pilot, not a new large study

**Complete current diagnostics first.** Use existing validation logits to compare identity versus positive global/primitive temperatures. Keep entire families together; for 40 contrast families with four per category, grouped four-fold cross-fitting is a reasonable exploratory design. Keep old source contexts grouped and preserve the declared old/new and primitive weighting. Fit temperatures on three folds, score the fourth, then aggregate out-of-fold predictions. Select neither temperature family nor checkpoint using the quarantined test. Reusing validation already seen during stopping still makes this developmental evidence, even with cross-fitting.

**If the audit passes and the user chooses the loss question, use one two-arm screen.** A concrete bounded option is to branch both arms from the saved 200-family state, restore identical optimizer/RNG state, and process exactly families 201–400 with the same old replay positions. Use CE versus CE+Brier with one fixed coefficient. Save the endpoint and one midpoint; stop at 200 additional complete family updates per arm. This tests the onset of the observed deterioration rather than trying to undo all 1,692 steps at once. It uses 4,000 new-judgment presentations and 600 old-question presentations per arm, plus the already-shared history. It is a continuation result conditional on v0.2 and the first 200 families.

Reuse the historical CE step-400 endpoint only if checkpoint, optimizer, ordered data, runtime and code equivalence are established; otherwise run a matched CE control after authorization. If a correctness fix changes model inputs or targets, the historical trajectory is no longer that control. Do not branch one arm from step 200 and another from step 1,692, or reset Adam in just one arm. Do not combine this screen with new teachers, Base initialization, larger capacity, a different learning rate, extra consistency, or data reweighting.

**Decision gates should be frozen before any such run.** Use the existing probability-based validation selection objective, all old retention limits, and per-primitive/whole-pair/whole-case accuracy. Compare each arm raw and with the same calibration procedure. Track Unknown precision/recall, high-confidence coverage and conditional error, risk versus coverage, error-detection AUROC, Score MAE, and source/rubric subsets. A drop in “wrong ≥.90” caused only by moving all answers below .90 is not a semantic success. Require evidence of gain beyond a calibrated CE control, and do not hide a failing semantic subgroup behind an aggregate NLL gain. Family-paired uncertainty intervals describe the corpus; one seed is insufficient for a training-variance claim. An ambiguous pilot result supports stopping, not extending the budget opportunistically.

The original contrast test and Jev outcomes stay quarantined through diagnostics and exploratory selection. Lock checkpoint, calibration fit, metrics, thresholds and comparison set before a later authorized final-test evaluation. Preserve its role as a single final comparison rather than using it to choose among this matrix. New follow-on data/target questions need their own independently authored development/calibration split; this review does not authorize replacing or rewriting the existing test.

## Why neither “hard labels” nor “use RL” is a sufficient diagnosis

These are algebraic deductions for the current finite-outcome setup, not new empirical findings:

1. For true conditional class probabilities `q`, expected CE is minimized at `p=q`; expected full-vector Brier is `||p-q||²` plus a constant. Therefore hard outcomes can train distributions. Soft targets can reduce target sampling noise or transfer information, but they do not automatically improve semantics.
2. With a deterministic training target, CE+Brier still has its infimum at the correct one-hot vector. It changes training dynamics, not the ideal certainty of that labeled example. Its potential benefit is empirical. For binary full-vector Brier, the logit derivative is `4(p-y)p(1-p)`, whereas BCE's derivative is `p-y`; pure Brier can barely correct a confidently wrong saturated logit.
3. In a fixed action space with observed class distribution `q`, expected correctness reward is `Σ_i π_i q_i`. Its optimum concentrates on a most-likely action rather than requiring `π=q`. That distinguishes decision optimization from honest probability reporting. Enumerating a small action space can eliminate rollout noise but does not make that objective proper.
4. Confidence-only negative squared error for a freely chosen answer has optimal expected reward `-s(1-s)`. An answer known to be wrong with reported confidence zero scores as well as an answer known to be correct with confidence one. Full-vector supervised Brier against a fixed gold event does not let the model choose the event being scored. RLCR's added correctness term addresses this different decision problem.

The v0.2 warm start also limits causal interpretation. The earlier [initialization comparison](../REVISED_INITIALIZATION.md) matched additional updates, not lifetime exposure: v0.2 had 5,000 historical updates, while the fresh arm did not. “Fresh Qwen” there retained Qwen's own post-training; it was not a pretraining-only Base model. Keep initialization fixed for the small loss screen. A Base-versus-post-trained comparison, or a capacity change, can be a later experiment with explicit interface-learning and exposure accounting if the bounded diagnostics warrant it.

The pre-training literature review and this pause provide no public, reproducible Jev reward recipe, nor evidence that Jev's behavior is attributable to RL alone. TypeSafe's public [primer](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/machine-learning-primer) and [launch description](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev), preserved in the prior source snapshots, disclose a broader training/architecture direction. They cannot identify the cause of OpenJev's plateau.

## Addendum: completed calibration and Score coverage evidence

The independent calibration review verifies that cross-fitted primitive temperatures lower latest-checkpoint new NLL from 1.5498 to .5072 and broad NLL from .3577 to .2324, with zero class-label changes. This is meaningful probability improvement on already-inspected validation, not improved semantic ranking or a deployed calibrator. New Score mean absolute error changes from .58881 to .59600 under that transform, so it does not improve every metric.

The pipeline audit found that the 20,000 **new contrast training** Score questions contain 10,400 two-level rubrics and 9,600 three-level rubrics, with no four- or five-level rubrics. This reviewer independently checked that all 160 contrast-validation Score questions use four levels. The difference is a concrete training/development coverage mismatch, not a normalization bug.

It is also **not globally unseen cardinality**: this reviewer reconstructed the existing canonical training replay selection and found 800 three-level wine-quality Score questions and 800 four-level oracle-policy Score questions. The v0.2 warm start and replay therefore supply other-domain four-level experience. The open question is transfer of that capability to the independently authored new evidence/rubric systems. Candidate count, outcome/rubric meanings, authoring style and prior stage mappings remain coupled; missing new-domain four-level examples are not established as the sole cause of the Score errors, much less of errors in other primitives.

This evidence raises the priority of a **small hard-label data-coverage ablation** above interpreting an objective-only change as the cure for semantic accuracy. If training is later authorized, include independently reviewed four-level new-domain rubrics, meaningful positive examples for every supplied level, same-evidence rubric changes, and source/time/entity/action admissibility controls. Verify the exact rendered candidate units. Compare with a hard-CE control under the same starting state, family presentations, replay schedule and selection rules. Record candidate counts, tokens and wall time because adding rubric levels changes compute even when update counts match. Do not change the objective, initialization or model size in that comparison, and do not manufacture extra levels or contrasts solely to meet counts.

The earlier 200-update CE/Brier screen remains a bounded way to test a different question—whether loss emphasis improves probability quality beyond temperature calibration—but it cannot isolate or repair the newly identified coverage shift by itself. Choose the next question rather than combining both changes. Keep all original held-out tests and Jev outcomes quarantined until the separate selection decision.
