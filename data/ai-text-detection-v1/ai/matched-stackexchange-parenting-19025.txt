I really appreciate that you're thinking carefully about this, but I want to gently push back on a few parts of the plan, as someone who thinks a lot about child development too.

**On cutting off all media and pop culture**
Banning TV, movies, pop music, and magazines entirely doesn't just remove "bad influences," it also removes shared reference points she'll need to connect with peers. Kids who are completely unfamiliar with what their friends are talking about often end up feeling isolated rather than protected, and that isolation can breed resentment toward the parent later, especially once she's old enough to notice what she's missing.

**On being her only educational source**
There's real value in structured critical thinking and one-on-one mentorship, but exposure to differing perspectives is actually essential for developing critical thinking in the first place. If you're her only teacher and the only source of ideas, she won't have much opportunity to encounter, challenge, or push back against views different from your own, which somewhat undercuts the "critical thinking" goal itself.

**What I'd suggest instead**
- Allow moderated exposure to media and pop culture, rather than a total ban, so she can relate to peers while you help her think critically about what she consumes.
- Consider hybrid education: home-based learning paired with some outside instruction, group classes, tutors, or co-ops, so she's exposed to other teaching styles and viewpoints.
- Focus your "no screens" energy on quality and moderation rather than complete prohibition, which tends to backfire once kids get older and gain more independence.

Ultimately, protecting a child from outside influence entirely often produces the opposite of intended independence, since she'll have had no practice navigating those influences herself before facing them without you around.
