Nikola Tesla was a Serbian-American inventor and electrical engineer best known for his groundbreaking work on alternating current (AC) power systems, which remain the foundation of modern electrical infrastructure. Born in 1856 in what was then the Austrian Empire, Tesla displayed an early aptitude for mathematics and physics, though he never completed a formal engineering degree.

After gaining experience working in the telephone industry and later for the Continental Edison Company in Europe, Tesla emigrated to the United States in 1884, where he briefly worked for Thomas Edison before striking out on his own. The two inventors famously clashed over the merits of direct current (DC), which Edison championed, versus alternating current, which Tesla believed was far more efficient for transmitting electricity over long distances. This rivalry, later dubbed the "War of the Currents," was largely settled in Tesla's favor after his AC system, licensed to George Westinghouse, was adopted for major infrastructure projects, including the Niagara Falls power station.

Beyond his work on AC power, Tesla was a prolific and often eccentric inventor. He conducted early experiments with wireless communication, X-ray imaging, and radio-controlled devices, and famously demonstrated a remote-controlled boat decades before such technology became mainstream. His most ambitious project, the Wardenclyffe Tower, aimed to enable wireless transmission of electricity and information across the globe, but the venture ultimately collapsed due to lack of funding.

Despite his brilliance, Tesla struggled financially for much of his later life and died in 1943, relatively obscure compared to contemporaries like Edison. In the decades since, however, his reputation has undergone a dramatic revival, and he is now widely celebrated as one of the most visionary and influential inventors in history, with numerous technologies, companies, and even a unit of magnetic flux density named in his honor.
