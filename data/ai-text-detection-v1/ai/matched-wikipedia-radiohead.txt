so Radiohead, if you're not already familiar, formed way back in 1985 in a town called Abingdon in England, though they didn't really blow up until the 90s. the lineup's stayed basically the same forever too, which is kind of rare, Thom Yorke on vocals, the Greenwood brothers (Jonny on guitar and all sorts of other instruments, Colin on bass), Ed O'Brien on guitar, and Phil Selway on drums.

their first big single was "Creep," and honestly a lot of people still only know them for that song, which is funny because the band themselves got kind of tired of it for a while. but then things got interesting. "The Bends" showed they had more range than one hit, and then "OK Computer" in 97 is the one that really cemented them as, like, a serious band, not just another 90s alt-rock act. that album gets thrown around a lot in "best albums ever" conversations, and it's usually not an exaggeration.

what's wild is what they did next. instead of chasing that same sound, they basically threw it out and made "Kid A," which is way more electronic, glitchy, almost cold sounding compared to their earlier stuff. a lot of fans were confused at first honestly, but it ended up getting ranked as one of the best albums of the decade by a bunch of major outlets.

they've also been kind of ahead of the curve business-wise, releasing "In Rainbows" as a pay-what-you-want download back in 2007, which was a pretty bold move at the time and got talked about almost as much as the music itself. by now they've sold tens of millions of records and got inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019, so yeah, they turned out fine.
