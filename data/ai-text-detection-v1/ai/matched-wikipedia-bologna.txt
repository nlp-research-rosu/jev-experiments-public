Bologna is a city in northern Italy and the capital of the Emilia-Romagna region. With a metropolitan population of roughly one million people, it is one of the largest urban centers in the country and sits at an important crossroads between the Adriatic coast and the Po Valley.

The city's history stretches back well over two thousand years. It was originally an Etruscan settlement before becoming a Roman colony under the name Bononia, and it later grew into a powerful medieval commune. Bologna is perhaps best known as the home of the oldest university in the Western world, founded in 1088, which has shaped the city's identity as a center of learning ever since. That academic tradition still defines Bologna today: a large share of its residents are students, giving the city a youthful, international character.

Architecturally, Bologna is distinguished by its extensive porticoes, covered walkways that line many of its streets and stretch for tens of kilometers in total. The historic center also features a cluster of medieval towers, the most famous being the Two Towers, along with grand piazzas and red-brick buildings that have earned the city the nickname "La Rossa," or "the red one." Bologna is equally famous for its cuisine, being the origin of dishes like tortellini and the meat sauce known internationally as Bolognese, which has helped make it a culinary destination in its own right.

Economically, Bologna remains a hub for manufacturing, food production, and trade fairs, and it consistently ranks among Italy's wealthiest and most livable cities. Its combination of medieval charm, academic prestige, and modern industry continues to attract visitors and new residents alike.
