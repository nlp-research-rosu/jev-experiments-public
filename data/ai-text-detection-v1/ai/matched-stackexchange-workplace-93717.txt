okay so here's the thing, technically you're not being paid for 40 hours of sitting there, you're being paid to produce a certain amount of correct work. time is really just a proxy for that, not the actual goal. if it were purely about clocking hours, it wouldn't matter at all how many mistakes you made, but it clearly does matter, which tells you results are what's actually being measured, even if "hours" is the number written on your paycheck.

but where it gets murky is the part where you deliberately slowed yourself down or added in fake errors just to make it look like you were working a full shift. that part i'd actually push back on. it's not really about whether you're "allowed" to work fast, it's that you're intentionally creating a false impression of effort, which is a different thing than just being efficient. if your output is genuinely correct and complete, working fast isn't dishonest. faking mistakes or padding time to hide how fast you actually are, that's the part that crosses into shaky territory.

practically speaking though, i don't think telling them would blow up in your face. most likely they'd have no real way to verify or fully understand what you did even if you explained it, and they're not going to fire someone who's clearly good at the job just because it turns out they're fast. but bringing it up would probably open a conversation you may not really want to have, about redistributing work, changing expectations, whatever.

anyway, ethics and contracts aren't the same thing. a contract is just whatever got negotiated between two sides with unequal leverage, it doesn't automatically define what's "right," it just defines what you agreed to.
