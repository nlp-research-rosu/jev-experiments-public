**Sharing the Canyon Safely: What to Know About Mountain Lions**

Grand Canyon National Park is home to a healthy population of mountain lions, and while sightings are rare, encounters are always a possibility for anyone hiking, camping, or exploring the park's more remote trails. Understanding a little bit about mountain lion behavior can go a long way toward keeping both you and these magnificent animals safe.

**Why lions and people share the same spaces**
Mountain lions require large territories and abundant prey, and much of the terrain they rely on overlaps with areas frequented by hikers and campers. Park biologists have tracked several lions using GPS collars over the years, gathering valuable data on their movement patterns, hunting habits, and how they respond to human activity. This research helps the park develop better guidance for reducing the chances of a dangerous encounter.

**Tips to avoid an encounter**
- Hike in groups whenever possible, and make noise as you move through dense brush or low-visibility areas.
- Keep young children close, ideally within arm's reach, rather than letting them run ahead on the trail.
- Stay alert during dawn, dusk, and nighttime hours, when mountain lions tend to be most active.

**If you do encounter a mountain lion**
Should you come across a mountain lion that doesn't immediately move away, remain calm and follow these steps:
- Pick up small children immediately.
- Face the lion directly and back away slowly. Never turn your back or run.
- Make yourself appear as large as possible, raise your arms, open your jacket, and speak loudly and firmly.
- If the lion continues to approach, throw rocks, sticks, or anything within reach to discourage it.

Fatal mountain lion attacks remain extremely rare in North America, and Grand Canyon has never recorded one. With a bit of awareness and the right response, visitors and lions can continue to safely share this remarkable landscape.
