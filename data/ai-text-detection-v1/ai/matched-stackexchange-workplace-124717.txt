Honestly, this sounds like a clear win for you, even if your manager doesn't see it that way yet.

You did exactly what was asked: you were told to improve scores, and you improved them dramatically. If the concern now is that you moved too fast or accomplished it in a way that wasn't originally anticipated, that's a communication and expectation-setting problem on their end, not a failure on yours.

A few thoughts on how to handle it:

- Document what you did. Write up a short summary of the approach you took and the measurable results, this is useful both for defending your work internally and for your own resume later.
- Ask directly what the actual concern is. Is it that the improvement seems "too easy," that it exposes some issue with how the metric works, or something else entirely? Understanding the real objection will help you respond appropriately instead of guessing.
- Don't be afraid to advocate for yourself. If leadership is uncomfortable with how quickly you hit the target, that's worth a calm, professional conversation, not something you should quietly absorb as a personal failure.

And separately: yes, absolutely update your resume. Taking a metric from a weak baseline to near-mastery is a legitimate, quantifiable achievement regardless of how your current employer reacts to it internally. Even if this particular situation is frustrating, having concrete, measurable results like this is exactly the kind of thing that stands out to future employers, so don't let an awkward internal reaction diminish how you present the accomplishment going forward.
