honestly, i think most people have completely lost the art of walking without a destination. we treat it like exercise now, something scheduled, something to be gotten over with, thirty minutes on a treadmill or a brisk loop around the block before getting back to the "real" business of the day. but that's not walking, not really, that's just moving your legs while your mind stays parked exactly where it was before you left.

real walking, the kind worth doing, has nothing to do with burning calories or hitting some step count on a watch. it's closer to a kind of wandering attentiveness, going out with no fixed plan and letting the road or the trail decide something for you instead of the other way around. there's an old saying about certain travelers being the only ones who truly rest while moving, chewing over the day slowly instead of rushing through it, and i think that's basically the whole point.

you also can't really fake this by walking indoors or on some track. something about being outside, actually exposed to weather and unpredictable ground, changes the whole experience. i've noticed this myself, the walks that actually clear my head are never the ones with a fixed loop and a known ending, they're the ones where i genuinely don't know which turn i'm going to take next.

so if you only ever walk for exercise, on a set route, at a set pace, you're getting the physical benefit sure, but you're missing basically the entire reason people have always valued walking as something more than transportation.
