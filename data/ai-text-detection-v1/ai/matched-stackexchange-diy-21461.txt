Great question! Washers might look like a simple, almost trivial piece of hardware, but they actually serve several distinct engineering purposes, and figuring out which one applies depends on the situation. Here's a rundown of the most common uses:

**1. Preventing loosening (lock washers)**
Some washers are specifically designed to resist vibration-induced loosening. These include split-ring, star, and wave designs, which create spring tension or biting edges that grip the mating surface and help keep a nut or bolt from backing out over time. They're typically placed under whichever component is more likely to rotate loose, often the nut.

**2. Protecting surfaces**
A plain flat washer can protect the material being fastened from damage caused by the rotating action of a nut or bolt head during tightening. This matters most with softer materials like aluminum, brass, or plastic, where direct contact with a turning fastener could gouge or mar the surface.

**3. Distributing load**
When fastening thin or soft material, a wider washer helps spread the clamping force of the bolt across a larger area, reducing the risk of the material deforming, cracking, or having the fastener pull straight through.

**4. Preventing washer deformation**
In some high-load situations, a large washer might itself start to dish or "mushroom" under pressure. In these cases, a second, smaller washer is sometimes added on top of the larger one to help distribute force more evenly and prevent that kind of warping.

So really, there's no single universal answer, the right washer (or combination of washers) depends entirely on what you're trying to achieve: preventing loosening, protecting a surface, spreading load, or some combination of the three!
