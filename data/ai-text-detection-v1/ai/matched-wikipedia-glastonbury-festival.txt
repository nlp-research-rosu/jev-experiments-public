Few events capture the spirit of British summer quite like Glastonbury Festival! Held on Worthy Farm in Pilton, Somerset, this five-day extravaganza has grown from a modest gathering into one of the largest and most iconic performing arts festivals on the planet, drawing crowds of around 200,000 people each time it runs.

**A festival rooted in counterculture**
Glastonbury's roots trace back to 1970, when farmer Michael Eavis, inspired by a Led Zeppelin concert he'd attended at the Bath Festival, decided to host his own event. What began as a small, free-spirited gathering has since blossomed into a sprawling celebration of music, theatre, comedy, circus performance, and more, all while holding onto the hippie and free-festival ethos that shaped its earliest days. Areas like the Green Fields still nod to that heritage, offering spaces dedicated to healing, sustainability, and alternative culture.

**More than just music**
While headline performances on the legendary Pyramid Stage tend to grab the headlines, part of the magic of Glastonbury lies in its sheer scale and variety. Thousands of acts perform across dozens of stages scattered throughout the site, and attendees can wander from a cabaret tent to a silent disco to a circus big top in the space of an afternoon. The festival famously operates almost entirely on volunteer labor and has raised millions of pounds for charitable causes over the decades.

**A break every few years**
Interestingly, Glastonbury takes deliberate "fallow years" roughly every five years, giving the land, the local community, and the organizers a much-needed rest. Far from diminishing its appeal, this rhythm has only added to the festival's mystique, making each return that much more anticipated by fans across the world.
