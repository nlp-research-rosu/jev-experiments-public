The key distinction here is between a genuine invitation and what's sometimes called a "soft" or vague suggestion. A real invitation typically includes enough specific detail that it could actually be acted on, things like a time, a place, or at least a clear proposal that the other person could say yes or no to. "Do you want to grab coffee sometime this week?" counts as an invitation because it can be followed up with concrete planning.

By contrast, something like "we should hang out sometime" or "you should visit me if you're ever in town" is often more of a social nicety than an actual invitation. It signals friendliness and openness, but it doesn't commit either person to anything, and both parties usually understand this implicitly. Responding to this kind of statement with "sure, when?" can sometimes come across as putting the other person on the spot, since they may not have intended it as a concrete offer in the first place.

That said, context and culture matter a lot here. In some cultures and social circles, vague statements like this are essentially just pleasantries, similar to saying "we should catch up sometime," and pushing for specifics can feel awkward or overly formal. In others, people are more direct, and a loosely worded suggestion is genuinely meant to be followed up on.

If you're ever unsure whether an invitation is real, a good approach is to respond with mild enthusiasm and leave the door open without forcing the issue, something like "I'd love that, let me know if you're serious and we can figure out a time." This way, you're not awkwardly calling out a vague offer, but you're also making it easy for the other person to follow through if they meant it.
