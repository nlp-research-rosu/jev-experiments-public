okay so poutine. if you've never had it, it's basically fries, cheese curds, and gravy all piled together, and honestly it sounds kind of weird until you actually try it and then it just makes sense. it's from Quebec, showed up sometime in the late 1950s, and there's this whole argument between a bunch of small towns about who actually invented it first. nobody's ever really settled that one and probably never will.

what's kind of funny is that for a long time poutine had a bad reputation. people looked down on it, treated it like some greasy backwoods snack, the kind of thing you'd only eat late at night after a few beers. and there's actually a bit of a sting to that history too, since some of the mockery got tied up with looking down on Quebec culture in general, not just the food itself.

but somewhere along the way that flipped completely. now poutine's treated like a point of pride, almost a symbol of Quebec identity, and it's spread everywhere from Ontario to New England to random food trucks you'd never expect. there are literally poutine festivals now, in Montreal, Toronto, even Chicago, which says a lot about how far it traveled from being a diner snack nobody respected.

the classic version uses fresh cheese curds, and that part actually matters a lot, cheese curds only stay "squeaky" for a short window after they're made, so old-school poutine spots were pretty limited by that. once places like fast food chains started serving it, some of them cut corners with worse cheese, which honestly didn't do the dish's reputation any favors for a while. but real poutine, done right, is one of those things that's simple but really hard to get exactly right.
