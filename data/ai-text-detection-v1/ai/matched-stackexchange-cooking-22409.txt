Yes, this is actually a known trick, and it works surprisingly well, though I'd recommend a few precautions before you try it.

The basic idea relies on the fact that a dishwasher's rinse cycle maintains a fairly consistent water temperature, which makes it a passable substitute for a sous-vide circulator in a pinch. Salmon typically cooks well at around 50-55°C, and many dishwashers run their main wash or rinse cycle in a similar range, though this varies quite a bit by model, so results aren't perfectly predictable.

To try it yourself, seal the salmon tightly in a vacuum bag or a well-sealed heavy-duty zip-top bag, pressing out as much air as possible using the water displacement method if you don't have a vacuum sealer. Place the bagged fish on the top rack, away from the direct spray of the detergent dispenser, and run a full cycle without soap, ideally using the "heated dry" or "sanitize" setting if your machine has one, since these tend to run hotter and more consistently.

A few important caveats: this method is much less precise than an actual sous-vide setup, since dishwashers aren't designed to hold a specific target temperature and their cycles vary by brand, model, and even how full the machine is. There's also a real risk of your bag leaking or shifting during the wash, so double-bagging is a good idea. And obviously, run an empty cycle beforehand if there's any chance of leftover detergent residue.

That said, plenty of people have reported good results, myself included. It's more of a fun kitchen experiment than a reliable everyday method, but if you're curious and don't own an immersion circulator, it's worth trying at least once.
