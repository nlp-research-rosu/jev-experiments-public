Siemens AG is a German multinational conglomerate headquartered in Munich, and it ranks among the largest industrial manufacturing companies in Europe. The company operates across several major sectors, and its reach extends into markets around the world through subsidiaries and joint ventures.

**Origins and early growth**
Siemens was founded in 1847 by Werner von Siemens and Johann Georg Halske, initially as a telegraph manufacturing business. Their early telegraph technology, which used a needle-based pointer system rather than Morse code, gave the company a technical edge that helped it expand rapidly. Within a few decades, Siemens had built long-distance telegraph lines connecting major European cities and even stretching as far as India, cementing its reputation as a leader in communications infrastructure.

**Expansion into electrical engineering**
The company's fortunes grew further after Werner von Siemens described the dynamo-electric principle in 1867, a discovery that helped pave the way for practical electric power generation. Siemens went on to build some of the earliest electric lighting systems and expanded into electric trains, becoming a pioneer of the emerging electrical engineering industry. Offices opened across Europe and eventually in Japan, reflecting the company's increasingly global ambitions.

**Modern operations**
Today, Siemens' main divisions include industrial automation, energy, healthcare technology, and infrastructure. Its healthcare unit, Siemens Healthineers, is a major producer of medical diagnostic equipment and represents one of the company's most profitable segments. Siemens employs several hundred thousand people worldwide and generates tens of billions of euros in annual revenue, making it a bellwether for the broader European industrial sector and a component of major stock indices like the Euro Stoxx 50.

From its origins as a telegraph workshop to its current status as an industrial giant, Siemens' history reflects the broader arc of technological progress in Europe over the past 175 years.
