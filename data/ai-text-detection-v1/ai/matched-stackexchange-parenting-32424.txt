A few things stand out here that are worth thinking through carefully.

First, on ownership: if your son contributed to paying for the device, that's a strong indicator that it was understood to be his property, not something you retained ownership of. Taking it and keeping it, even temporarily as a consequence for breaking a house rule, doesn't transfer ownership to you, similar to how a teacher confiscating a phone at school doesn't make the phone the school's property. Once he moved out, the situation becomes even less ambiguous: it's his device, being kept in what is now his own household.

Second, on how he got it back: taking something from a drawer in your home is a boundary issue, certainly, but it's a very different category of action than "stealing," especially when the item in question was already his. It sounds more like he was recovering something he considered rightfully his, after presumably feeling like ongoing conversations about it weren't going anywhere.

On the lying question: it's worth considering why lying felt like the safer option to him in that moment. If honesty tends to be met with conflict or punishment regardless of the circumstances, people, especially young adults still navigating their relationship with a parent, often calculate that deception carries a lower cost than the truth. That's not a great pattern, but it's a very human and common one, and it says as much about the dynamic between you as it does about him individually.

At this point, since he's now an adult living independently, I'd gently suggest that this particular issue may do more relationship damage than good to pursue further, and it might be better used as a lesson learned quietly rather than reopened directly.
