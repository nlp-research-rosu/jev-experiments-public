Cricket is a bat-and-ball sport played between two teams of eleven players, most commonly associated with countries that were once part of the British Empire, including England, India, Australia, Pakistan, and the West Indies. The game is played on a large oval field, with a rectangular strip in the center called the pitch, where most of the action takes place.

The basic aim of the game is for the batting team to score runs while the bowling team tries to dismiss the batters and restrict scoring. A run is scored when a batter hits the ball and both batters successfully cross to the opposite end of the pitch, though runs can also be awarded automatically if the ball is hit to the boundary. A batter can be dismissed in several ways, most commonly by being bowled, when the ball hits the wicket behind them, or by being caught, when a fielder catches the ball before it touches the ground after being struck.

Cricket is played in several formats, which differ mainly in length and structure. Test cricket, the oldest and most traditional format, can last up to five days, with each team batting twice. Limited-overs formats, such as One Day Internationals and the shorter Twenty20 format, restrict the number of overs bowled, making for faster-paced, more results-oriented matches that tend to draw larger television audiences.

The sport has a rich history dating back to 16th-century England, and it has since become deeply embedded in the cultural identity of many nations. Major international tournaments, such as the Cricket World Cup and various T20 leagues, now attract massive global viewership, and cricket remains one of the most widely followed sports in the world, trailing only football in terms of total fan base.
