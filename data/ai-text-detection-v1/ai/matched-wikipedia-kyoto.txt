Kyoto is a city in central Japan and the capital of Kyoto Prefecture, located in the Kansai region on the island of Honshu. With a population of roughly 1.4 million, it forms part of the greater Keihanshin metropolitan area alongside Osaka and Kobe, though it retains a distinct identity shaped by its long history as Japan's imperial capital.

For more than a thousand years, from 794 until 1869, Kyoto (originally known as Heian-kyō) served as the seat of the Japanese imperial court. During this era, the city developed into the country's political, cultural, and religious center, and much of its layout still reflects the grid pattern established when it was first designed. The imperial court eventually relocated to Tokyo in the 19th century following the Meiji Restoration, but Kyoto retained its status as Japan's cultural heart.

One of the most notable aspects of Kyoto's history is that it was largely spared from bombing during the Second World War, a decision reportedly influenced by its historical and cultural significance. As a result, the city preserves an unusually large number of pre-modern temples, shrines, and gardens compared to other major Japanese cities. Landmarks such as Kinkaku-ji (the Golden Pavilion), Kiyomizu-dera, and the Fushimi Inari shrine draw millions of visitors annually, and much of the historic core has been designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Beyond its temples, Kyoto is also a major center of education and research, home to Kyoto University, one of Japan's most prestigious institutions. The blend of deep historical roots and modern academic and cultural life continues to make Kyoto one of the most visited and celebrated cities in Japan.
