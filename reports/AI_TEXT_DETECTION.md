# Detecting AI-written text with Jev

Jev 1.13 (`jev-1.13.0`, via the TypeSafe API) can separate human from AI-written English prose on our
92-sample set with a held-out AUC of **0.966**, using one request per 300-word chunk and a four-weight
logistic formula over its answers. At the operating threshold, **0 of 44** human texts were flagged as
likely AI and **30 of 48** AI texts were. The set is small, all AI text comes from one model family, and
AI text written to sound casual was never flagged, so these numbers describe this sample set, not
detection in general.

This is the calibration behind the Slop Alarm browser extension. Code: `experiments/ai_text_detection/`.
Data: `data/ai-text-detection-v1/`. Raw responses and result tables: `reports/ai-text-detection-v1/`.
Every table below can be regenerated from the committed responses without an API key (see the
experiment README). Measured 2026-09-20, re-queried 2026-09-23.

## Setup

**Data.** 44 human texts fetched verbatim from sources published before November 2022: 15 Wikipedia
revisions from 2019, 20 Stack Exchange answers from 2012 to 2019, 8 Gutenberg essay excerpts, and 1 US
National Park Service page. 48 AI texts written by Claude models: 8 stereotyped assistant outputs and
40 texts matched one-to-one to a human sample by topic, genre and length. The matched texts use three
imagined prompt styles: 24 `default`, 9 `engaging`, and 7 `casual-disguised` ("write casually, like a
real person, avoid sounding like AI"). Details and licensing: `data/ai-text-detection-v1/README.md`.

**Questions.** Each chunk (at most 300 words) is sent as `state` with eight questions in one request:

| Id | Type | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| `ai_written` | Noul | Was this generated by an AI language model rather than written by a person? |
| `tells` | Score, 4 levels | How saturated is the text with stock AI habits (levels are descriptive situations) |
| `staging` | Noul | "Not X but Y" contrasts, dramatic one-line closers, announced run-ups |
| `rhythm` | Noul | Forced triads, dashes as the default connector, repeated openings |
| `inflation` | Noul | Stock vocabulary, significance claims, unnamed authorities |
| `formatting` | Noul | Bold labels on every item, Title Case headings, emoji markers |
| `chat_residue` | Noul | "I hope this helps", "Great question!", knowledge-cutoff disclaimers |
| `specifics` | Noul | First-hand specifics a person would have and a model would not |

The five habit families follow github.com/blader/humanizer, which distils Wikipedia's "Signs of AI
writing". Full wording: `experiments/ai_text_detection/core/questions.ts`.

**Scoring.** `p = sigmoid(-7.80 + 7.85·ai_written + 6.32·tells/3 + 2.91·staging - 2.54·specifics)` per
chunk. Chatbot residue at or above 0.9 floors `p` at 0.9. A sample's probability is the word-weighted
mean of its chunk probabilities. Verdicts: likely human below 0.30, likely AI from 0.75, very likely AI
from 0.90, unclear in between. The other four questions do not enter the score; they explain a verdict.

**Fitting and validation.** L2 logistic regression (λ = 0.1), fitted per chunk, with each sample
weighted equally and classes balanced (`eval/fit.mjs`). Validation is leave-one-group-out: a human
sample and its topic-matched AI twin are held out together, so neither a sibling chunk nor a same-topic
twin leaks into training. 123 chunks, 92 samples, 52 groups.

## Results

### Held-out accuracy

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---:|
| AUC | 0.966 |
| Human texts flagged likely AI or above (p ≥ 0.75) | 0/44 |
| One-sided 95% upper bound on that rate (Clopper-Pearson) | 6.6% |
| AI texts flagged likely AI or above | 30/48 (62.5%) |
| Human texts read as likely human (p < 0.30) | 30/44 (68.2%) |
| AI texts read as likely human | 2/48 (4.2%) |

The 0.75 threshold was chosen on these same samples as the lowest value where no held-out human sample
was flagged, so even the 6.6% bound is optimistic. At lower thresholds:

| Flag threshold | Human flagged | AI missed |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.50 | 2/44 | 4/48 |
| 0.60 | 2/44 | 6/48 |
| 0.70 | 1/44 | 16/48 |
| 0.75 | 0/44 | 18/48 |
| 0.80 | 0/44 | 23/48 |
| 0.90 | 0/44 | 28/48 |

What a flag is worth depends on how much of the checked text is AI. With the false-positive rate at its
upper bound and a 62.5% catch rate, 33% of "likely AI" flags would be right if 5% of checked text were
AI, 70% at 20%, and 91% at 50%.

In-sample (weights fitted on all 92, `eval.log`), the AUC is 0.977 and the verdict counts are:

| True label | Likely human | Unclear | Likely AI | Very likely AI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Human (44) | 32 | 12 | 0 | 0 |
| AI (48) | 1 | 17 | 9 | 21 |

### Which questions carry the signal

Per-signal AUC and means, in-sample (`eval.log`). `tells` is on Jev's 0 to 3 scale. An AUC below 0.5
means the signal is higher for human text.

| Signal | AUC alone | Human mean | AI mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `ai_written` | 0.919 | 0.535 | 0.829 |
| `tells` | 0.813 | 0.684 | 1.273 |
| `staging` | 0.631 | 0.249 | 0.346 |
| `inflation` | 0.611 | 0.148 | 0.267 |
| `formatting` | 0.569 | 0.089 | 0.143 |
| `chat_residue` | 0.482 | 0.055 | 0.096 |
| `rhythm` | 0.407 | 0.160 | 0.129 |
| `specifics` | 0.284 | 0.185 | 0.067 |

Held-out comparison of scored subsets (`feature-ablation.txt`):

| Scored questions | AUC | Human flagged at 0.75 | AI missed at 0.75 | Humans read human |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ai_written` | 0.914 | 0/44 | 19/48 | 47.7% |
| `tells` | 0.807 | 2/44 | 32/48 | 38.6% |
| `ai_written`, `tells` | 0.958 | 0/44 | 17/48 | 63.6% |
| + `specifics` | 0.962 | 0/44 | 17/48 | 63.6% |
| + `staging` (shipped) | 0.966 | 0/44 | 18/48 | 68.2% |
| all eight | 0.972 | 0/44 | 20/48 | 65.9% |

The direct question does most of the ranking, but on its own it is badly calibrated for formal human
prose. Its mean on human Wikipedia text is 0.74, close to the AI mean of 0.83, against 0.55 for Stack
Exchange and 0.12 for Gutenberg. Used alone, it flags 12/44 human texts at a 0.50 threshold and 7/44 at
0.60, where the four-signal formula flags 2/44 at both. The habits score moves the other way on
Wikipedia, which is plain and neutral, and that is what rescues those samples. All eight questions
together raise the AUC slightly but read fewer humans as human at the operating threshold, so the
shipped formula keeps four. The five family questions barely separate the classes on their own; their
value in the product is explaining a verdict.

| Group | n | Mean probability | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Human, Gutenberg | 8 | 0.024 | 100% read likely human |
| Human, Stack Exchange | 20 | 0.249 | 75% read likely human |
| Human, Wikipedia | 15 | 0.296 | 53% read likely human |
| AI, stereotyped | 8 | 0.985 | 100% flagged |
| AI, engaging | 9 | 0.926 | 100% flagged |
| AI, default | 24 | 0.768 | 54% flagged |
| AI, casual-disguised | 7 | 0.535 | **0% flagged** |

(In-sample. The single government page is omitted from the table: p = 0.075, likely human.)

Encyclopedic human prose is the hardest human case: nearly half of Wikipedia reads "unclear". AI text
prompted to sound casual defeats the detector at this threshold; six of seven read "unclear" and one reads
likely human. Detection works on text that shows its habits: about half of default assistant output and all
"engaging" output. It does not work on text written to hide them.

### Markdown is not the shortcut

Markdown appears in 19 of the 48 AI samples and in no human sample, so the detector could have learned
"markdown means AI". We stripped the markup from those 19 texts, keeping every word, and re-queried Jev
(`ablations/markdown-stripped`, `strip-markdown.mjs`).

| Mean over the 19 texts | With markdown | Stripped |
|---|---:|---:|
| Probability | 0.886 | 0.883 |
| `ai_written` | 0.873 | 0.871 |
| `tells` (0 to 3) | 1.457 | 1.461 |
| `staging` | 0.372 | 0.379 |
| `specifics` | 0.050 | 0.049 |
| `formatting` (not scored) | 0.242 | 0.156 |
| Flagged likely AI or above | 16 | 16, the same texts |

Only the formatting question noticed the change, and it is not part of the score.

### Repeatability

All 123 chunks were re-sent on 2026-09-23 with identical inputs (`rerun-2026-09-23/`), requesting the pinned
model id `jev-1.13.0` both times. Jev is close to repeatable but not bit-identical:

| Question | Identical values | Mean absolute change | Largest change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `ai_written` | 50/123 | 0.008 | 0.04 |
| `tells` (rescaled to 0 to 1) | 8/123 | 0.010 | 0.06 |
| `staging` | 51/123 | 0.009 | 0.05 |
| `rhythm` | 62/123 | 0.006 | 0.04 |
| `inflation` | 69/123 | 0.005 | 0.03 |
| `formatting` | 57/123 | 0.006 | 0.03 |
| `chat_residue` | 101/123 | 0.003 | 0.06 |
| `specifics` | 90/123 | 0.004 | 0.03 |

Sample probabilities moved by 0.012 on average and 0.055 at most. AUC was 0.976 against 0.977. Three
samples changed verdict, each within 0.021 of a band edge: two human texts went from 0.296 and 0.292
(likely human) to 0.317 and 0.313 (unclear), and one AI text went from 0.894 to 0.903 (likely AI to very
likely AI). No sample crossed the 0.75 flag threshold. Values that sit near a band edge should be read
as sitting on it.

### Cost

123 requests, 215,091 input tokens (about 1,750 per chunk, mostly the question text), $0.009 at
$0.042 per million input tokens. Output tokens are free. Single requests took 0.65 to 1.0 s in spot
checks; latency was not measured systematically.

## What changed during development

These points come from earlier runs whose responses are not in this branch, because the question set
changed afterwards. They explain the current design; they are not reproducible from these files.

- An earlier version asked three questions per chunk and six more only when the first three looked
  suspicious. One request with all nine questions cost about 1,900 input tokens and returned in under
  a second, so the two stages were merged into one call.
- A 4-level `voice` Score (from "one person's voice" to "uniformly polished default prose") was
  dropped. It rated neutral Wikipedia prose as AI-like, measuring formality rather than authorship.
- The `tells` Score first asked "how often" the habits appear. TypeSafe documents that Jev does not
  count reliably, so it now asks which descriptive level fits.
- Weights were first fitted on per-sample averaged signals and validated leave-one-out, while
  production scores chunks and then averages probabilities. Refitting per chunk and holding out each
  topic-matched pair together lowered held-out AUC from 0.98 to 0.97, and one held-out human sample
  reached 0.70, which moved the threshold to 0.75. The numbers above use the per-chunk, grouped fit.

## Limits

- 92 samples. Zero false positives out of 44 still allows a true rate of several percent.
- All AI text comes from Claude models. ChatGPT, Gemini and open models are untested.
- No news, personal blogs, social media or non-native English writers on the human side.
- AI samples are shorter (199 to 319 words) and more uniform in length than human ones (168 to 399).
- The operating threshold was chosen on the evaluation data.
- English only. Chunks under 40 words are not judged.
